Services

What I do

From a single question to an embedded role — with four decades of judgment behind it.

Fractional CTO

Technology strategy, mentoring for development teams and their leaders, and the 2 a.m. questions of a first-time founder — aligning technical and business decisions all the way to a successful exit. I’ve been a startup CTO myself, through an NYSE IPO. I’ve already made most of these mistakes, and shipped anyway.

Rapid prototyping, at AI speed

You have a next-generation idea and a team that can’t be distracted from the current product. I take it from whiteboard to working, demoable software in weeks, using AI wherever it actually helps, and solid engineering wherever vibe coding isn’t good enough.

AI workflow coaching

Your developers are already using AI tools and getting uneven results. I work with the team on what actually changes: which parts of the workflow agents genuinely speed up, which ones they quietly break, and the review habits that catch the difference. I do this on my own projects every day — and write about what I find.

Technical due diligence

Two sides. Buying an investment, an acquisition, or a rebuild: you need to know what the stack, the architecture, and the team can actually carry. Being examined — raising, or selling — you’d rather hear it first from someone on your side: I find what their reviewer will find, while there’s still time to fix it. Built, bought, and retired every generation of this.

Research & technology decisions

Should we adopt this, when, and how much of it — or: we’re stuck here, and the obvious answers aren’t working. I take the question as a single scoped piece of work and come back with a written answer: what has actually been made to work, what it costs to run, and where it breaks. Most recently, transparent and trust-preserving ranking algorithms for a social-discussion platform.

Patent & technical IP guidance

I help you find your product’s inventive bits and work alongside your patent attorneys through the whole arc — provisionals, utility applications, office actions, allowance. I’m a named inventor on four US patents myself — I’ve sat on your side of the table.

Track record

A flag on every wave

דגל (degel) is Hebrew for “flag”. I’ve planted one on every technology wave since 1980 — not watching from shore, but shipping commercial software inside each shift.

1980–1990

TCP/IP, Lisp machines & the first AI wave

At MIT in 1980, I worked with the Laboratory for Computer Science team implementing the Internet’s TCP/IP protocols. Then straight into the first AI wave: at Symbolics — maker of the Lisp machine, and holder of the Internet’s first registered domain (symbolics.com, 1985) — I built the Command Processor, parts of the window system, and a Lisp-powered constraint-propagation spreadsheet far ahead of the PC spreadsheets of its day. Then I co-founded International Lisp Associates.

After moving to Israel: image-processing and object-recognition toolkits on Lisp machines for MBT–Israel Aircraft Industries and several AI and CAD startups.

Era artifacts
I still keep a Lisp machine emulator running. Some loves last.
1990–1996

Windows, machine vision & translation

I wrote major portions of a paint and drawing program for NTT, Japan’s telecom giant, and built core components — plus some genuinely advanced C++ — for Cognex machine vision. I ported a commercial machine-translation system to English and Japanese Windows, and wrote medical software that McKesson later acquired. Much of the language-technology work ran through Basis Technology — a client relationship that continues to this day.

Era artifacts
1996–2001

The first Internet boom

I co-founded 2AM Inc. as VP Technology and directed its core Creation engine — the scripting and runtime environment for a massively distributed, peer-to-peer game platform that served over 250,000 users in the dial-up era.

Era artifacts
2001–2010

Mobile, before the iPhone

I ported Sun’s J2ME runtime to the first Symbian smartphones, built ICQ’s and AOL’s mobile messaging clients, and created Orange UK’s push-to-talk client. We shipped dozens of Symbian and J2ME apps — including a Nokia 9210 device driver that Nokia said couldn’t be written (we wrote it), and portions of QuickOffice, which Google later acquired and folded into Google Docs.

Symbian named Degel a Platinum Partner, and Degel-built apps took home Handango Champion awards in 2003 and 2004. I co-founded Vringo as CTO, steered its mobile products through the arrival of the modern smartphone, and helped guide it to an NYSE IPO.

Era artifacts
2010–2023

The modern web & NLP

Full-stack product work for startups and established clients — including a Clojure-based biological-computation system for the National University of Singapore. As Principal Software Engineer at Basis Technology, I designed and built the Adaptation Studio and Identity Studio NLP front ends. I also pushed the company into ChatGPT and LLM techniques early — my on-ramp to the next wave.

Era artifacts
2023–now

AI-augmented development

I work inside this one daily. Most of that time goes to MarketBuzzr — I’m co-founder and technical lead, building its multi-stage LLM pipeline and the product around it — and to the client work below: workflow automation, operations software, LLM-first prototypes.

The fun spills into weekends. One produced a 13,000-line Scheme interpreter in Rust — a language I’d never used — passing all 1,225 tests of the standard conformance suite; the next, a Z80 and CP/M emulator that boots the genuine 1979 operating system and runs Zork, WordStar, Turbo Pascal, and many other great applications from back then.

About

David Goldfarb

Degel Software is me — an independent software consultant in Beit Shemesh, Israel — and, when a project needs it, the like-minded senior developers I’ve worked with for years. I’ve kept the company deliberately small since 1988 so that everything it ships meets one quality standard: mine.

If you ask me to build something that seems wrong, I’ll tell you before I build it. You get a shared issue tracker you can read any time, schedules I don’t pad, and progress reports that tell you when something slipped. My goal is your project’s success, not billable hours.

I’ve been writing software since the late 1970s and I still like doing it — which is most of why the company never grew past the size where I could read every line. Clients across the decades have ranged from AOL, Sun, and Intel to Israel Aircraft Industries and the National University of Singapore.
